Visual-to-auditory sensory substitution

Every letter is a sound.
Every sound has a shape.

OVAL is an auditory orthography developed for blind readers. Each character is a small arrangement of dots and vertical lines, and the EyeMusic algorithm turns that arrangement into sound: left to right is time, high to low is pitch, and colour is the instrument you hear. Write a word below in Hebrew or in transcription, then follow the sweep as it reads.

The alphabet

Twenty-two characters

Every character combines up to four dots and vertical lines, borrowing the spatial patterns of Braille and the temporal patterns of Morse. Lines run vertically rather than horizontally so that a line is heard as a chord instead of a long tone. Tap any character to hear it. The eleven marked with a ring are the ones participants were trained on.

  • White is a choir
  • Blue is a trumpet
  • Red is a piano
How it works

Reading with your ears

OVAL was designed so that characters stay distinguishable once a shape has become sound. This page is a web port of the original Android app and plays the same recordings.

A sweep line

Each character is scanned left to right, so the marks are heard in the order they are drawn.

Height is pitch

Vertical position maps onto a pentatonic scale, and a line becomes a chord.

Colour is timbre

A redundant cue that made readers both faster and more accurate than monochrome ones.

Seventeen speeds

Every character was recorded at 17 tempos, from eight steps slower to eight steps faster.
